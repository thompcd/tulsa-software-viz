# The 3.2x Advantage

## Hook
"An engineer in Austin needs $110K to live comfortably. In Tulsa? $34K. Here's what that means for hiring."

## Target Audience
- Tech recruiters
- Remote workers considering relocation
- Companies evaluating office locations
- Tulsa Remote program candidates

## Visualization Concept
Side-by-side city comparison showing purchasing power parity. Interactive "salary translator" — input a salary in one city, see equivalent in Tulsa.

Options:
- Split-screen comparison infographic
- Scatter plot: salary vs cost of living by city
- "What $X buys you" visual comparison

## Key Data Points
| City | Typical Home | 2BR Rent | Min Family Income |
|------|-------------|----------|-------------------|
| Tulsa | $179,100 | $1,020 | $34,200 |
| Austin | $577,400 | $1,720 | $110,520 |
| Denver | $576,000 | $1,910 | $110,520 |
| National Avg | $338,100 | $1,430 | — |

- Tulsa homes 47% cheaper than national average
- Tulsa homes 69% cheaper than Austin/Denver
- At Austin prices, you could buy 3.2 Tulsa homes
- Tulsa rent 28.7% below national average

## Data Sources
- BestPlaces.net (cost of living data)
- NAM regional comparisons

## CTA Angle
Cost-of-living arbitrage for tech companies and remote workers.
