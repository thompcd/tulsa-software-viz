Tulsa vs Austin:
The Next Tech Hub?

Austin promises big salaries. But when you adjust for the cost of actually living there, the math tells a different story.

By Tulsa Software · February 2026

Scroll to explore ↓

The Illusion of the Big Salary

A software developer in Austin earns a mean salary of $139,390 per year, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

In Tulsa, the mean for computer and mathematical occupations is $94,682.

Source: BLS Occupational Employment & Wage Statistics, May 2024

That's a $44,708 gap. Case closed, right?

Not so fast. Austin's cost of living is 57% higher than Tulsa's.

Source: BestPlaces.net Cost of Living Calculator, 2026

When you adjust Austin's $139,390 salary for purchasing power, it's equivalent to just $88,761 in Tulsa dollars.

That means Tulsa's $94,682 actually buys you $5,921 more in real purchasing power.

The "lower" salary wins.

Source: BestPlaces.net COL Calculator, 2026

What Your Money Actually Buys

A typical home in Austin costs $577,400. In Tulsa, it's $179,100.

Austin homes cost 3.2× more than Tulsa homes.

Source: BestPlaces.net, 2026

Renting isn't much better. A two-bedroom apartment runs $1,720/month in Austin versus $1,020/month in Tulsa.

That's $8,400 a year you could put toward savings, student loans, or actually enjoying your life.

Source: BestPlaces.net, 2026

And here's the kicker: 52% of Tulsa residents own their home. In Austin, it's only 44.4%.

Higher salaries don't mean much if you can never afford to buy.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau ACS 2023

Tulsa Isn't Just Cheap — It's Building

Tulsa isn't a flyover city waiting to be discovered. It's a metro area with 412,000 people, Fortune 500 headquarters, and a manufacturing sector that punches above its weight.

Source: U.S. Census ACS 2023; Tulsa's Future

Manufacturing makes up 8.1% of Tulsa's employment — well above the 5.7% national average. Aerospace, energy, and advanced manufacturing are anchors.

Major employers include American Airlines, Williams Companies, BOK Financial, and QuikTrip.

Source: BLS OEWS May 2024; BestPlaces.net

Tech giants are paying attention too. Google and Amazon have operations in the Tulsa region, alongside international aerospace firms.

Tulsa's unemployment rate sits at 4.7% — lower than Austin's 5.1% and the national average of 6.0%.

Source: Tulsa's Future; BestPlaces.net, 2026

And your commute? 18.7 minutes average in Tulsa versus 24 minutes in Austin. That's 46 hours a year of your life back.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau ACS 2023

The Punchline

Let's put it all together. When you compare the numbers side by side, Tulsa wins on the metrics that matter most for quality of life.

Austin has stronger job growth projections and a larger tech ecosystem. Those are real advantages.

But Tulsa offers something Austin increasingly can't: a life you can actually afford — on a tech salary that goes further than you think.

The next tech hub isn't always the most expensive one.
Sometimes it's the smartest one.
